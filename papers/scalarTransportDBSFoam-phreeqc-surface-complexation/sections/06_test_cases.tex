\section{Test Cases}

Table~\ref{tab:simParams} lists the full set of simulation parameters for each
test case. Three distinct surface-complexation chemistries are demonstrated,
each chosen to highlight a different aspect of nonlinear multi-species coupling
that cannot be captured by a simple retardation factor, and each motivated by
a distinct class of subsurface application:
(i)~Pb$^{2+}$ sorption on a two-site Dzombak--Morel hydrous ferric oxide (HFO)
coating for the 2D fracture case, representative of heavy-metal retardation in
fractured rock systems and permeable reactive barrier remediation;
(ii)~selective Mn$^{2+}$ uptake from Mg$^{2+}$-rich brine on a two-site HFO
surface for the Kelvin-cell 3D case, representative of critical-metal capture
in microporous industrial sorbent media; and
(iii)~Sr$^{2+}$ leachate invading Ca$^{2+}$-bearing limestone formation water,
representing a radioactive-waste deep geological repository (DGR) scenario,
for the Estaillades 3D case.
The validated coupling, image-derived interface-area construction, and
two-scale DBS reactive-surface representation are then applied to progressively richer
2D and 3D reactive-transport problems, including media in which the intrinsic
micropore contribution to $a_{\mathrm{surf}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:aSurf})
contributes to uptake.
In all cases the mobile-water chemistry mask is
$\theta \le \theta_{\mathrm{mask}} = 10^{-3}$
and PHREEQC thermodynamic data are drawn from the GeoChem.dat database
distributed with GeoChemFoam.
The 1D case uses a prescribed uniform Darcy velocity $U_D = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$;
the 2D and 3D image-based cases use a velocity field obtained from a
prior \texttt{simpleDBSFoam} flow calculation with pressure drop
$\Delta P = 5\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{Pa}$ and kinematic viscosity
$\nu = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}$.

\subsection{Simple 2D Image-Based Case: Pb$^{2+}$ on Dzombak--Morel HFO}

The 2D demonstration uses the GeoChemFoam benchmark fracture-network image
\nolinkurl{4StraightFractures}. The segmented domain has dimensions
$500 \times 500 \times 1$ with two labels corresponding to micropore ($1$) and
pore ($2$). The computational grid is $250 \times 250 \times 1$.
This case provides a geometrically transparent setting to study heavy-metal
retardation by iron-oxide coatings on fracture walls, directly relevant to
Pb$^{2+}$ and other heavy-metal contaminants migrating through iron-rich
fractured aquifers, acid mine drainage pathways, and engineered permeable
reactive barriers where HFO is the primary sorbent phase.

The chemistry represents equilibrium Pb$^{2+}$ sorption on hydrous ferric
oxide (HFO) using the Dzombak--Morel two-site model
\citep{dzombak1990surface}. The strong and weak sites have intrinsic
$\log K$ values of $+4.65$ and $+0.30$, respectively, and site densities of
$2 \times 10^{-4}$ and $6 \times 10^{-3}$~mol~m$^{-2}$. The inlet
Pb$^{2+}$ concentration is
$C_{\mathrm{in}} = 10^{-3}$~mol~L$^{-1}$ at pH~7, while the initial domain
is essentially Pb-free.

At pH~7, HFO sorption retards dissolved Pb$^{2+}$ relative to conservative
chloride, although the Pb$^{2+}$ plume spans most of the domain by
$t=20000$~s. The total reactive-area field combines the reconstructed
micropore--pore interface with an intrinsic unresolved surface-area density
$a_{\mathrm{surf,intr}} = 10^{5}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ assigned to the
label-1 microporous material. Its end-member porosity is $\epsilon=0.4$,
with phase-fraction weighting applied in mixed computational cells.

Figure~\ref{fig:2DFractures} shows the segmented geometry,
Pb$^{2+}/C_{\mathrm{in}}$, and the strong-site occupancy
Hfo$_s$\allowbreak OPb$^+$/\allowbreak Hfo$_{s,\mathrm{tot}}$ at $t=20000$~s. Dissolved Pb$^{2+}$
concentrations are higher along the open fracture channels than in the adjacent
microporous matrix. Strong-site occupancy is greatest near the inlet and is
locally enhanced in microporous material adjacent to fracture intersections.

\subsection{Simple 3D Image-Based Case: Selective Mn$^{2+}$ Capture from Mg$^{2+}$-Rich Brine}

The simple 3D case uses a Kelvin-cell image dataset with dimensions
$200 \times 200 \times 200$ and voxel size $10~\mu\mathrm{m}$, where
label $1$ denotes pore and label $2$ denotes a connected microporous lattice.
Transport is solved in DBS form through both labels; the micropore cells carry
$\theta = 0.4$ and the intrinsic reactive surface area.
This case demonstrates competitive multi-species sorption in a fully
three-dimensional geometry with a cell-based reactive surface inventory: a
trace target metal is transported through a Mg$^{2+}$-rich background brine and
competes for HFO surface sites. The scenario represents Mn$^{2+}$ capture from
a high-hardness process or mine-water stream in an open-cell sorbent medium.
Although the conditional affinities are moderate at neutral pH, Mn$^{2+}$ has
stronger HFO complexation than Mg$^{2+}$ in the selected reaction set, so
breakthrough and surface loading are controlled by both advection through the
Kelvin-cell geometry and competitive exchange on the reactive coating.

The chemistry uses the same Dzombak--Morel two-site HFO model as the 2D Pb
case, with Mn$^{2+}$ and Mg$^{2+}$ competing on weak sites and Mn$^{2+}$ also
complexing with strong sites:
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{Hfo_sOH} + \mathrm{Mn^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Hfo_sOMn^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -0.40 \\
    \mathrm{Hfo_wOH} + \mathrm{Mn^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Hfo_wOMn^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -3.50 \\
    \mathrm{Hfo_wOH} + \mathrm{Mg^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Hfo_wOMg^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -4.60
\end{align}
The initial and inlet Mg$^{2+}$ concentration is
$10^{-3}$~mol~L$^{-1}$; Mn$^{2+}$ is absent initially and enters at
$10^{-5}$~mol~L$^{-1}$. Surface sites are equilibrated initially with the
Mg-bearing background water, after which incoming Mn$^{2+}$ accumulates on
the HFO sites and displaces part of the Mg inventory.
The strong- and weak-site densities are $2\times10^{-4}$ and
$6\times10^{-3}$~mol~m$^{-2}$, respectively, and
$a_{\mathrm{surf,intr}} = 10^{5}$~m$^{-1}$ in the micropore phase.

Figure~\ref{fig:3DKelvin} shows the geometry and 3D volume renders of both
competing dissolved species and their corresponding adsorbed-amount changes at
$t = 120$~s.
Figure~\ref{fig:KelvinTemporal} shows the temporal evolution of pore-volume
weighted aqueous Mn$^{2+}$, the excess aqueous Mg$^{2+}$ released above the
background concentration, and the surface-area-weighted strong- plus weak-site
HFO loadings over the full 120~s simulation. The adsorbed-amount panel
quantifies the competitive exchange: HFO-Mn accumulates behind the front while
the initially equilibrated HFO-Mg inventory decreases where the Mn-bearing
water has contacted the sorbent.

\subsection{Large 3D Porous-Media Case: Sr$^{2+}$ DGR Scenario}

The large 3D porous-medium case uses an Estaillades-style three-phase
segmented image \citep{menke2021upscaling} with dimensions
$500 \times 500 \times 500$ and voxel size $4~\mu\mathrm{m}$. The three phases
correspond to pore ($1$), micropore ($2$), and solid ($3$). The simulation is
run at the native $500 \times 500 \times 500$ voxel resolution on 64 MPI
ranks. This case demonstrates nonlinear chromatographic exchange in a
geologically realistic pore structure, motivated by radionuclide migration
from nuclear-waste deep geological repositories.

The chemistry models Sr$^{2+}$ leachate from a waste package invading
Ca$^{2+}$-bearing limestone formation water. Before transport begins, the
surface sites are equilibrated locally with a 1~mM Ca$^{2+}$ solution at pH~7,
so the initial sorbed inventory is Ca-dominated. The two competing surface
reactions are:
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{Surf_a H} + \mathrm{Ca^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Surf_a Ca^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -5.85 \\
    \mathrm{Surf_a H} + \mathrm{Sr^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Surf_a Sr^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -5.5
\end{align}
Sr$^{2+}$ (site density $2.4\times10^{-6}$~mol~m$^{-2}$) binds slightly more
strongly than Ca$^{2+}$. The initial and outlet-side condition is
Ca$^{2+}$ = $10^{-3}$~mol~L$^{-1}$, Sr$^{2+}$ = 0; the inlet carries
Sr$^{2+}$ = $10^{-4}$~mol~L$^{-1}$, Ca$^{2+}$ = 0 at pH~7.

This produces a classic chromatographic exchange pattern: a Ca$^{2+}$
desorption wave precedes the Sr$^{2+}$ adsorption front, and the effective
retardation of Sr$^{2+}$ is lower than the single-species prediction because
Ca$^{2+}$ still competes for sites ahead of the front. Over the saved output
sequence ($t = 0, 20, \ldots, 120$~s), the porosity-weighted mean
Sr$^{2+}$ concentration rises from effectively zero to $0.865$
$C_{\mathrm{in}}$, while the corresponding Ca$^{2+}$ concentration falls
from $1.000$ to $0.095$ $C_{\mathrm{init}}$. Over the same interval,
\mbox{$\Delta$\texttt{Surf\_aSr+}} increases monotonically while
\mbox{$\Delta$\texttt{Surf\_aCa+}} becomes progressively more negative,
quantifying the net replacement of Ca$^{2+}$ by Sr$^{2+}$ on the reactive
surface. Near-solid cells (\mbox{$\theta \le 10^{-3}$}) are masked from the
reactive-surface field.

Figure~\ref{fig:3DEstaillades} shows the geometry and 3D volume renders of the
dissolved and adsorbed fields at $t = 40$~s.
Figure~\ref{fig:EstailladesTemporal} summarises the corresponding weighted
exchange history.
