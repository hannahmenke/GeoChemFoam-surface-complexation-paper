\section{Reactive-Area Construction and Capacity Mapping}

The cell-centred reactive-area density is the sum of an image-scale interface
term and an intrinsic sub-voxel term. For the interface term, a segmented owner
phase is converted to a smoothed occupancy field, a sub-voxel boundary is
extracted, and the resulting line or surface measure is accumulated in the
finite-volume cells that carry the reactive surface. The designated owner is
only the image label whose visible boundary is counted; it can represent a
microporous grain, dense solid, or another reactive phase.

In three dimensions, the reconstruction extracts an isosurface and deposits
triangle areas into their owning cells. In quasi-two-dimensional images, it
extracts an isoline in the image plane, multiplies its length by the physical
out-of-plane thickness, and assigns that area to the corresponding DBS cells.
The two routes therefore return the same field type and units,
m$^2$\,m$^{-3}_{\mathrm{bulk}}$. The field is constructed before flow and is
held fixed throughout reactive transport.

The interface term excludes roughness and pore walls below the segmented image
resolution. An intrinsic surface density $s_j$ (m$^2$\,m$^{-3}$) can therefore
be assigned independently to each image label $j$. Multiplication by its local
phase fraction $f_j$ preserves mixed-cell volume, and summation over phases
gives $a_{\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{intrinsic}}=\sum_jf_js_j$. The same label
may contribute both its reconstructed outer boundary and its intrinsic area.
Values inferred per phase volume or grain mass must first be converted to a
bulk-volume basis, for example using phase fraction and grain density. This
step is essential because porosity is not a conversion factor for a field
already expressed per bulk volume.

PhreeqcRM receives area per representative bulk litre. Accordingly,
$a_{\mathrm{surf}}$ in m$^2$\,m$^{-3}$ is divided by $10^3$ and is not divided
by the mobile-water fraction. For surface master $k$, multiplication by the
site density $s_k$ (mol\,m$^{-2}$) gives the site capacity per representative
bulk volume. OpenFOAM stores amount in kmol, so a dictionary site-density field
in kmol\,m$^{-2}$ is multiplied numerically by $10^3$ when interpreted as
mol\,m$^{-2}$. This sequence makes the units explicit:
\[
\left[\frac{\mathrm{m}^2}{\mathrm{m}^3_{\mathrm{bulk}}}\right]
\left[\frac{\mathrm{mol}}{\mathrm{m}^2}\right]
=\left[\frac{\mathrm{mol}}{\mathrm{m}^3_{\mathrm{bulk}}}\right].
\]

The porosity field defines mobile-water storage in OpenFOAM and solution volume
in PHREEQC. Cells below \texttt{epsChemistryMin} are excluded from the fixed
chemistry mapping, and their surface contribution is
suppressed. The reported calculations use $10^{-3}$ as this threshold.

\section{Discrete PHREEQCRM Coupling}

All aqueous components, porosity, total reactive area, and surface-species
fields are stored at cell centres. Darcy velocity is represented by face
fluxes. Implicit Euler time integration and first-order upwind advection are
used, with Gauss linear diffusion and non-orthogonality correction. The
diffusion operator is linear; negative aqueous values are clipped before
chemistry under the configured relative mass-addition limit.
The surface-complexation cases select \texttt{mixtureType phreeqcMixture} in
\texttt{constant/thermoPhysicalProperties}.

At construction, every active finite-volume cell is registered as a PHREEQC
solution. Reactive cells also receive a PHREEQC \texttt{SURFACE} definition
whose area and site capacities are derived from the fixed fields. The active
mapping does not change during the run. Porosity is, however, reread and set
for every mapped PHREEQC cell at each chemistry correction. Reconstruction is
needed only if a threshold crossing changes the active-cell map or its topology;
changing the fixed area capacity likewise requires reconstruction.

One timestep performs the following operations: (1) advance each aqueous
species with the conservative DBS equation; (2) transfer aqueous and surface
fields to PHREEQCRM; (3) reread porosity and set it in every mapped PHREEQC cell;
(4) clip negative
aqueous inputs to zero while measuring the mass introduced by clipping and
abort if the configured relative bound is exceeded; (5) execute
\texttt{runCells()}; and (6) transfer equilibrated aqueous and adsorbed states
back to the finite-volume fields. Chemistry is therefore a cell-local update
after transport, not a redistributed volumetric source or a face flux.

Each OpenFOAM process constructs PhreeqcRM with one worker thread. Domain
decomposition provides process-level parallelism, and \texttt{runCells()}
advances only the chemistry cells held by that rank. The application cases use
the decompositions in Table~\ref{tab:si-simParams}.

\subsection{Checkpoint and restart lifecycle}

Unless restoration is requested, chemistry is initialised from the written
aqueous and surface fields at the selected start time. A restart can instead
set \texttt{phreeqcRestoreCheckpoint} and load a complete matched PHREEQCRM
state. Restoration fails if its dump or completion marker is missing or
inconsistent. At an output time, a checkpoint-enabled run first invalidates
the old completion marker collectively, writes a new rank-local dump, writes
the finite-volume fields, and publishes a completion marker only after the
field write succeeds. Thus a crash before publication cannot authenticate a
partially updated field--chemistry pair. The PHREEQCRM dump and marker must be
retained with the corresponding finite-volume time directory; the
\texttt{Surf\_*} fields alone do not reconstruct the complete internal state.

\section{Analytical and Closed-Batch Protocols}

The one-dimensional benchmark uses an $80\times1\times1$ mesh, uniform
$\theta=0.5$, $a_{\mathrm{surf}}=10^5$~m$^{-1}$,
$U_D=10^{-6}$~m\,s$^{-1}$, $D=10^{-11}$~m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, timestep 0.25~s,
and final time 80~s. Two dilute batch equilibrations provide the secant Henry
coefficient $K_s=\Delta\Gamma/\Delta C=4.253\times10^{-6}$~m. The inventory
then becomes $(\theta+a_{\mathrm{surf}}K_s)C$ and gives
$R=1+a_{\mathrm{surf}}K_s/\theta=1.851$.

The spatial comparison at 10~s uses the cell average of the analytical
advection--diffusion profile rather than its point value. The outlet comparison
uses a finite-domain solution on $[0,L]$ with the same Dirichlet inlet and
Neumann outlet as the OpenFOAM case. The semi-infinite Ogata--Banks solution is
retained as a boundary-condition diagnostic \citep{ogata1961solution}; it
predicts later breakthrough because solute can diffuse beyond $L$. Predicted
advective breakthrough is $L\theta/U_D$ without surface reaction and
$L\theta R/U_D$ with surface reaction, giving 20.0 and 37.0~s.

The closed-batch test removes flow and boundary flux. For initial aqueous
concentration $C_0$, linear equilibrium gives
\[
C_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{\theta C_0}
{\theta+a_{\mathrm{surf}}K_s},\qquad
\mathcal M=\theta C+a_{\mathrm{surf}}\Gamma=\mathrm{constant}.
\]
The tested $C_0$ values are $10^{-9}$ and $2\times10^{-9}$~mol\,L$^{-1}$.
After one chemistry update, relative inventory drifts are
$8.9\times10^{-10}$ and $1.1\times10^{-10}$.

A quasi-two-dimensional circular post is compared with $2\pi Rh$, where $h$

quasi-two-dimensional path reconstructs a contour whereas the

\subsection{Reactive image micromodel}

The solid-grain micromodel compares two numerical representations of the same
segmented geometry. The body-fitted case maps each wall-face area to its
adjacent chemistry cell. DBS maps its reconstructed interface area to the
corresponding Cartesian cells. Pressure drops are calibrated separately so
that inlet volumetric fluxes agree within $0.004\%$. The comparison therefore
assesses integrated reactive area, its spatial distribution, aqueous transport,
and sorbed inventory rather than exact wall placement.

At 200~s the body-fitted and DBS areas are $1.65\times10^{-5}$ and
$1.67\times10^{-5}$~m$^2$, respectively. The DBS-minus-reference differences
are $+1.48\%$ in area, $-0.07\%$ in dissolved inventory, and $+1.68\%$ in
sorbed inventory; the streamwise aqueous-profile L1 error is $0.48\%$.

\section{Short Two-Scale Reconstruction Sensitivity}

The two-scale calculation is a matched short-interval sensitivity test
within the DBS representation. It is not a DBS-versus-resolved transport
comparison. Both cases use a $3600\times1200\times1$ grid with $5~\mu$m cells,
16 MPI ranks, $Pe=1$, $D=10^{-9}$~m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, and prescribed Darcy velocity
$5.46297\times10^{-5}$~m\,s$^{-1}$. The surface field is the sole intended
input difference; 495 other input files were verified as matched.

Both short cases use their case-local \texttt{GeoChem.dat} together with the
same illustrative Li-selective sorbent chemistry. The surface reactions are
$\mathrm{Sor\_aH}+\mathrm{Li^+}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Sor\_aLi}+\mathrm{H^+}$
($\log K=-5.8$), and the corresponding Na$^+$, K$^+$, and Mg$^{2+}$ reactions
have $\log K=-7.2$, $-7.0$, and $-7.4$, respectively. Initially Li-free brine
contains 0.10~M Na, 0.01~M K, 0.01~M Mg, $10^{-12}$~M Li, and 0.13~M Cl at pH
7; the inflow changes Li to $10^{-3}$~M and Cl to 0.131~M. These chemistry
details describe only the reported short $Pe=1$ sensitivity.

Three common checkpoints at 0.0058517566, 0.11703513, and 0.99564208~s meet
the predefined material-response limits. The maximum absolute differences
are $0.000647\%$ in integrated area, $0.000703\%$ in aqueous Li inventory,
$0.2110\%$ in sorbed Li inventory, $0.00172\%$ in aqueous normalised L1,
$0.5147\%$ in sorbed normalised L1, and $0.03993\%$ in fixed-reference-area
sorbed-response L2. Aqueous and sorbed $x_{50}$ shifts are zero. The native-cell
sorbed inventory-density L2 is 3.61--4.49\%, dominated by the deliberate
relocation of area; pointwise identity is therefore not inferred. The accepted
last reported checkpoint covers 0.489\% of the configured 203.5305~s horizon, which bounds the
claim to early material-response agreement.
