\abstract{We present a mass-conservative Darcy-Brinkman-Stokes (DBS)
formulation for reactive transport with cell-based surface complexation in
image-based porous media, coupled to PHREEQC and implemented in GeoChemFoam,
an OpenFOAM-based toolbox. Dissolved species are transported in the physical
mobile-water fraction, adsorbed species are stored as conservative cell-scale
surface inventories, and each reactive cell is coupled to PHREEQC. Reactive
surface area is prescribed independently as a cell field, so that internal
area left unresolved by segmentation of microporous phases can be represented
while DBS flow and transport are retained in the resolved pore space.

The implementation is verified with a test suite that isolates the coupling
of transport and chemistry from the construction of reactive surface area.
Aqueous and adsorbed mass balance is evaluated against a one-dimensional
analytical retardation benchmark and closed-system batch calculations. The
smooth-interface surface-area algorithms are then tested on idealised two- and
three-dimensional geometries, and reactive transport in heterogeneous pore
structures is examined in image-based fracture-network and Estaillades
limestone cases.

The applications comprise Pb$^{2+}$ retardation by hydrous ferric oxide
coatings in a two-dimensional fracture network, competitive Mn$^{2+}$/Mg$^{2+}$
exchange in a three-dimensional Kelvin-cell sorbent, and Sr$^{2+}$/Ca$^{2+}$
exchange in Estaillades limestone. In the fracture-network and limestone cases,
the formulation yields spatially resolved aqueous and adsorbed inventories even
where reactive surface area is only partly resolved at the image scale. The
framework therefore supports conservative surface-complexation modelling in
both resolved and unresolved pore structures, with relevance to contaminant
retention, selective ion capture, and radionuclide transport.}
