\begin{abstract}
We present a conservative Darcy-Brinkman-Stokes (DBS) formulation for
PHREEQC-coupled reactive transport with cell-based surface complexation in
image-based porous media. The formulation transports dissolved species in the
physical mobile-water fraction, stores adsorbed species as conservative
cell-scale surface inventories, and couples each reactive cell to PHREEQC
without ad hoc clipping or source redistribution. This provides a direct way to
represent unresolved internal reactive area in segmented microporous phases,
while retaining the DBS description of flow and transport through resolved pore
space. The approach is motivated by applications in groundwater contaminant
retention, critical-mineral recovery, direct lithium extraction, and
radionuclide transport in geological materials.

The implementation is assessed through a validation sequence that separates
conservative coupling, surface-area construction, and two-scale upscaling. A
1D analytical retardation benchmark and closed-system batch tests verify the
transport--chemistry balance. Circular-post and sphere benchmarks isolate the
2D and 3D smooth-interface surface-area algorithms. Image-based micromodel and
Estaillades external-surface comparisons then evaluate DBS
\texttt{smoothSurface} against resolved-wall \texttt{snappyHexMesh}
references, including the resulting sorbed inventories.
A resolved-to-DBS two-scale micromodel benchmark tests Li$^{+}$ capture from
Na--K--Mg brine as a direct-lithium-extraction analogue. Across P\'{e}clet
numbers from $0.1$ to $10$, aqueous and sorbed inventory differences remain
below $0.9\%$ and profile errors remain below $5.8\%$.

The validated formulation is applied to three image-based reactive-transport
cases spanning distinct applications: Pb$^{2+}$ retardation by HFO coatings in
a 2D fracture network for heavy-metal remediation, selective Mn$^{2+}$ capture
from Mg$^{2+}$-rich brine in a 3D Kelvin-cell sorbent geometry for
critical-mineral separation, and Sr$^{2+}$/Ca$^{2+}$ exchange in Estaillades
limestone as a radionuclide-transport scenario for nuclear-waste deep
geological repositories. Across these cases, the method produces spatially
resolved aqueous and adsorbed inventories in geometries where the reactive
surface area is partly unresolved at the image scale. The results support
conservative DBS surface-complexation coupling as a practical framework for
multi-scale reactive transport in segmented porous-media images.
\end{abstract}
