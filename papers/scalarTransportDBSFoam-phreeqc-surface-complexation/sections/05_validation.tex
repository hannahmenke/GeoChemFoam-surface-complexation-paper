\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

The validation ladder separates conservative chemistry--transport coupling
from the construction and use of reactive area. The one-dimensional tests have
no geometric ambiguity. Controlled interfaces then test the two- and
three-dimensional area reconstruction, followed by reactive image comparisons
against body-fitted resolved-wall calculations. The resolved-wall cases test
only image-scale interface area: they neither define the target DBS
formulation nor represent intrinsic sub-voxel area. Online Resource~1 provides
the complete protocols, secondary panels, and metric definitions.

\subsection{Analytical Retardation and Batch Balance}

The one-dimensional benchmark uses uniform $\theta=0.5$,
$a_{\mathrm{surf}}=10^5$~m$^{-1}$, $D=10^{-11}$~m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, and an
$80\times1\times1$ mesh. Two dilute PHREEQC batch equilibrations give
$K_s=4.253\times10^{-6}$~m and hence $R=1.851$. At $t=10$~s, the
surface-enabled profile agrees with the matching cell-averaged analytical
solution to $1.34\%$ in relative L1 norm and $2.73\%$ in $L_\infty$; its
total-equivalent inventory differs by $1.19\%$. The aqueous-only errors are
$1.08\%$, $5.97\%$, and $0.58\%$, respectively.

The profile comparison uses cell averages of the analytical solution, so the
reference and finite-volume result are compared on the same support. The
surface-enabled front is delayed relative to the aqueous-only front because the
calibrated capacity increases the local inventory per aqueous concentration.
This tests the complete chain from the PHREEQC batch slope through the
area-to-capacity mapping to the transported field; it is not only a comparison
of equilibrium concentrations. The two initial concentrations in the batch
test further check that the transfer between the aqueous and adsorbed fields
does not create or remove total Ca$^{2+}$ when boundary fluxes are absent.

The outlet response extends the test to $t=80$~s. Surface-enabled breakthrough
occurs at $37.0$~s compared with $20.0$~s without surface reaction, giving the
predicted factor of $1.851$. The finite-domain solution matches the simulated
Dirichlet-inlet/Neumann-outlet problem; the semi-infinite Ogata--Banks solution
is later because it permits diffusion beyond the simulated outlet
\citep{ogata1961solution}. In two closed batches initialised at
$10^{-9}$ and $2\times10^{-9}$~mol\,L$^{-1}$, relative Ca$^{2+}$ inventory
drifts are $8.9\times10^{-10}$ and $1.1\times10^{-10}$. These results verify
the area-to-capacity mapping independently of image geometry.

Figure~\ref{fig:1DRetardation} makes the spatial comparison explicit for the
aqueous-only and surface-enabled cases and also shows the calculated adsorbed
profile. Figure~\ref{fig:1DBreakthrough} extends the comparison to the outlet:
the predicted advective breakthrough times are 20.0~s without surface reaction
and 37.0~s with it. The finite-domain analytical solution uses the same
Dirichlet inlet and Neumann outlet as the simulation. The semi-infinite
Ogata--Banks curve is retained as a boundary-condition diagnostic, because its
allowance for diffusion beyond the outlet delays breakthrough relative to the
finite domain \citep{ogata1961solution}.

\subsection{Image-Derived Area and Unresolved-Area Response}

Controlled circular-post and sphere tests isolate the geometric construction.
The quasi-two-dimensional contour remains within $0.2\%$ of $2\pi Rh$ across
the tested resolutions. The three-dimensional reconstruction differs from
$4\pi R^2$ by $-0.06\%$ and $+0.87\%$ at $R/\Delta x=8$ and 16, comparable
with the body-fitted values of $-0.65\%$ and $-0.50\%$.

Reactive image tests assess whether those integrated areas remain meaningful
when coupled to transport. In a solid-grain micromodel at $t=200$~s, DBS and
the separately flux-matched resolved-wall case differ by $+1.48\%$ in area,
$-0.07\%$ in dissolved inventory, $+1.68\%$ in sorbed inventory, and $0.48\%$
in the streamwise aqueous-profile L1 norm. On a $128^3$ Estaillades crop at
$t=2.5$~s, the Darcy velocities agree within $1.12\%$; the DBS external area
is $1.52\%$ greater and sorbed Sr$^{2+}$ inventory is $1.84\%$ lower. Area
maps are compared after binning resolved-wall faces onto the image grid, but
integrals and inventories are evaluated on each method's native support.

These comparisons have complementary roles. The controlled post and sphere
tests measure the reconstruction against known smooth geometries in both the
quasi-two-dimensional contour and three-dimensional isosurface branches. The
reactive image tests then ask whether the reconstructed area gives a consistent
integrated chemical response when a resolved wall can be simulated. The
body-fitted calculation is a comparator for the visible interface term, not a
replacement for the cell-based formulation: its wall faces are supported on a
different mesh and it contains no intrinsic sub-voxel area. The reported
agreement therefore supports integrated area and inventory on these tractable
geometries, rather than asserting cell-by-cell geometric equivalence.

A separate short $Pe=1$ sensitivity test addresses the surface reconstruction
used by the unresolved-matrix case. It compares two DBS reconstructions, not a
DBS calculation with a resolved transport reference. Across three matched
checkpoints ending at $0.99564208$~s, the largest changes are $0.000647\%$ in
integrated area, $0.000703\%$ in aqueous Li inventory, $0.2110\%$ in sorbed Li
inventory, $0.00172\%$ in aqueous normalised L1, and $0.5147\%$ in sorbed
normalised L1. The reported interval spans $0.489\%$ of the configured horizon,
so these results establish only short-interval material-response agreement.

The sensitivity is retained because it isolates an input choice relevant to
the unresolved-matrix representation while keeping the transport model fixed.
The unchanged aqueous inventory and low integrated sorbed-inventory difference
show that the two accepted reconstructions produce closely matched early
responses. The larger native-cell sorbed-density difference is expected when
area is deliberately relocated between cells and is not used as evidence of
pointwise agreement. No extrapolation to the remaining simulation horizon or
to a resolved reference is made from this short interval.
