\section{Conclusions}

We formulated cell-based PHREEQC surface complexation within conservative DBS
reactive transport. A fixed mobile-water field defines aqueous accumulation,
transport, and PHREEQC solution volume, while a separate bulk-volume area field
combines resolved interface and intrinsic sub-voxel contributions. The paired
aqueous and surface inventories therefore retain a direct mass-balance
interpretation.

The implementation reproduces one-dimensional analytical retardation with
near-percent profile and inventory errors and conserves closed-batch inventory
to PHREEQC convergence. Controlled and image-based comparisons show
near-percent agreement between the reconstructed interface area and
body-fitted resolved-wall references. The fracture and Estaillades applications
then expose the intended mechanisms: rapid delivery through connected pores,
storage on unresolved matrix area, and chromatographic displacement under
competitive sorption.

The method addresses reactive area that lies below the image resolution
without forcing it onto an explicit mesh boundary. Its predictive accuracy
nevertheless depends on independently constrained intrinsic area and on the
assumption that this area is locally accessible. Extension to evolving area,
kinetic reactions, and higher-order coupling should therefore retain the
inventory tests and scale-separated validation used here.
