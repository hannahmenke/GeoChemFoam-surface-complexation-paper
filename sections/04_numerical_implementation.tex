\section{Numerical Implementation}

This section describes the discrete fields, the transport--chemistry coupling,
and the per-timestep algorithm used in the reported simulations. The
implementation couples conservative Darcy-Brinkman-Stokes (DBS) transport
with cell-centred PHREEQC chemistry and cell-based surface complexation in a
single operator-split formulation.

\subsection{Field Locations And Discretization}

The baseline discretization keeps all chemistry-bearing state cell-centered.
This avoids introducing an extra face reconstruction for the unresolved
surface-reaction source. The solution species, $\theta$,
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{num}}$, $a_{\mathrm{surf}}$, and the surface fields are
therefore stored at cell centres, whereas only the volumetric flux $\phi$
remains face-centred. Transport uses face interpolation only within the
conservative flux assembly, while the surface chemistry remains strictly
cell-local. The additive decomposition of $a_{\mathrm{surf}}$ into interface
and intrinsic contributions is defined in Section~\ref{sec:aSurf}
(Eqs.~\eqref{eq:asurf}--\eqref{eq:asurf_intrinsic}).

All simulations reported here use the following discretization. Time
integration uses the first-order implicit Euler scheme, consistent with the
first-order Lie splitting of the transport and chemistry operators. The
convective flux $\nabla\cdot(\phi C_i)$ is discretized with a bounded
first-order upwind scheme, which is monotone and free of spurious oscillations
on the unstructured image-based meshes used here. The diffusive term
$\nabla\cdot(\theta D_i \nabla C_i)$ uses a second-order Gauss linear scheme
with non-orthogonality correction. The combination gives a scheme that is
first-order accurate in both time and space for advection-dominated transport,
consistent with the overall first-order operator-split framework.

\subsection{PHREEQC Coupling}

The PHREEQC coupling is performed through \texttt{phreeqcDBSMixture}, which
wraps the PhreeqcRM interface \citep{parkhurst2015phreeqcrm}. At
initialisation, each computational cell is registered as a separate PHREEQC
solution. When surface complexation is active, a PHREEQC \texttt{SURFACE}
block is also created for each cell. The block specifies the total moles of
surface master sites as
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{sites}} = s_k \, \frac{a_{\mathrm{surf}}}{\theta},
\label{eq:nsites}
\end{equation}
where $s_k$ (mol\,m$^{-2}$) is the user-prescribed site density for surface
master species $k$, and $a_{\mathrm{surf}}/\theta$ is the surface area per
unit water volume (Eq.~\eqref{eq:asurf_water}). This initialises the total
reactive capacity of the surface in each cell consistently with the
field-level surface area.

The $a_{\mathrm{surf}}$ field is computed from the segmented image before the
simulation begins (Section~\ref{sec:aSurf}) and is held constant throughout
the reactive-transport calculation; it is not updated by the flow or chemistry
solvers. At each timestep, the current $a_{\mathrm{surf}}$ and $\theta$ are
used to pass the area per unit water volume to PhreeqcRM via
\texttt{setSurfaceAreaScale}, ensuring that the surface capacity seen by
PHREEQC remains consistent with the finite-volume fields even as $\theta$ may
vary spatially. The surface mode is optional and reduces exactly to
aqueous-only mode when $a_{\mathrm{surf}} = 0$ everywhere.

Cells with negligible mobile-water fraction ($\theta < \theta_{\min}$, where
$\theta_{\min}$ is a small threshold) remain chemically inactive in the
finite-volume fields. The PHREEQC backend retains a minimal internal water
volume for these cells only to maintain numerical well-posedness of the
chemistry module; their aqueous and surface states do not contribute to any
reported inventory.

The cell-local nature of the chemistry step means that all PHREEQC calls are
embarrassingly parallel across cells. PhreeqcRM distributes the cell
chemistry over available threads using OpenMP, so the chemistry cost scales
with the number of cores used for the transport solve.

\subsection{Per-Timestep Algorithm}

At each timestep the solver executes the following sequence. First, the face
flux $\phi$ is read or updated from the DBS flow solution. Second, each
aqueous transport equation is advanced in conservative DBS form using the
implicit Euler--upwind discretization described above.
Third, the updated aqueous concentrations and surface states are pushed into
PhreeqcRM. Fourth, PhreeqcRM executes the local equilibrium chemistry for all
active cells in parallel. Fifth, the updated aqueous and surface
concentrations are pulled back into the finite-volume fields. Finally, the
domain-integrated bulk inventory
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{total}} = \sum_{\mathrm{cells}} V_{\mathrm{cell}}
\left( \theta C + \frac{a_{\mathrm{surf}}}{\theta}\Gamma_{\mathrm{field}} \right)
\end{equation}
is computed and written, where the sum runs over all cells and $V_{\mathrm{cell}}$
is the cell volume. This integrated diagnostic is the primary conservation
check: in a closed domain it should remain constant to within discretisation
error, and in a flow-through domain it should balance the net flux through
the inlet and outlet faces.

The operator-split structure keeps
transport and chemistry individually testable and ensures that the
conservative structure of the aqueous transport step is not hidden inside a
composite implicit source term.

fluxes, while the reaction operator acts locally on the updated cell state
through PHREEQC.
