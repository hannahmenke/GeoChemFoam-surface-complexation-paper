\section{Validation}

\subsection{1D Analytical Retardation Benchmark}

The first validation target is the tracked 1D linear-retardation benchmark case
in the repository. This case provides the dilute analytical reference used to
verify the field-level retardation factor and the expected reduction to
retarded transport in the linear-equilibrium limit. The benchmark compares a
surface-enabled moving case, a matched aqueous-only moving case, and a dilute
batch secant used to estimate $K_{s,\mathrm{field}}$.

For the validated benchmark reported here,
$K_{s,\mathrm{field}} = 4.2412187733863601\times 10^{-6}$ and
$R_{\mathrm{field}} = 2.6964875093545437$. The mesh contains
$80 \times 1 \times 1$ cells and uses a species diffusion coefficient of
$10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$. The surface-enabled profile matches the
retarded advection--diffusion reference with a relative L1 error of $0.75\%$
and a relative $L_{\infty}$ error of $2.68\%$. The corresponding aqueous-only
profile matches its analytical advection--diffusion reference with a relative
L1 error of $1.87\%$ and a relative $L_{\infty}$ error of $8.44\%$. The
field-level total-equivalent inventory differs from the analytical limit by
$0.67\%$ for the surface-enabled run and by $2.41\%$ for the aqueous-only run.

These values are sufficient for the manuscript to claim that the production
solver reproduces the dilute field-level retardation limit of the current DBS
formulation. Figure 1 therefore focuses on the profile-level comparison rather
than a weak mean-only metric: the left panel shows the numerical cell averages
against cell-averaged analytical advection-diffusion references, and the right
panel shows the normalized adsorbed loading against the matching cell-averaged
linear-limit surface response.

\subsection{Controlled Numerical Validation}

In addition to the analytical benchmark, the solver has been checked against a
set of controlled numerical baselines, including the aqueous-only reduction,
the \mbox{$a_{\mathrm{surf}} = 0$} reduction, closed-system adsorption and
desorption tests, and decomposition, timestep, and mesh sensitivity studies on
the fixed 1D benchmark. Together, these checks constrain both the mathematical
correctness of the formulation and the operating envelope used in the later
image-based test cases.
