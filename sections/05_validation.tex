\section{Validation}\label{sec:validation}

The validation ladder separates four questions. The closed-batch and transient
1D benchmarks separately test conservative aqueous--surface state transfer and
coupled reactive transport independently of image geometry. Controlled
geometric tests then verify how the reactive surface-area field
$a_{\mathrm{surf}}$ is constructed from segmented images, and image-based
micromodel comparisons put that same construction to work in reactive
transport. A resolved-to-DBS multiscale micromodel benchmark closes the ladder
by testing whether the upscaled surface-area representation preserves
sorption-controlled transport when part of the image is treated as an
unresolved reactive matrix.

For the image-derived geometric benchmarks, \texttt{snappyHexMesh} provides the
resolved-wall reference: an established OpenFOAM route to a body-fitted mesh
from a segmented interface between pore and solid, on which reactive area and
surface inventory can be evaluated directly. Its practical range sets the range
of the comparison. Meshing becomes unreliable or fails for the segmented
volumes considered here once the image size reaches order $600^3$ voxels, and
the snapped mesh carries no unresolved DBS matrix volume or intrinsic sub-voxel
area as a cell-supported inventory. The DBS surface-area fields are therefore
compared against the resolved-wall area distribution and sorption response
wherever an explicit-wall reference can be generated.

For validation quantities compared without independent normalization, we
report the physical-measure-weighted relative $L_1$ discrepancy,
\begin{equation}
    E_1(q) = 100\,
    \frac{\sum_i w_i\lvert q_i-q_i^{\mathrm{ref}}\rvert}
         {\sum_i w_i\lvert q_i^{\mathrm{ref}}\rvert}.
    \label{eq:relativeL1}
\end{equation}
The weights $w_i$ represent the appropriate pore volume, bulk-cell volume,
interfacial area, or streamwise-bin width. When each $q_i$ is already an
integrated bin inventory, $w_i=1$. Quantities defined on different numerical
supports are conservatively projected onto common physical bins before
comparison.
For integrated inventories, $E_1$ quantifies relative disagreement in total
amount, while the signed relative difference indicates over- or
underprediction. Streamwise $E_1$ quantifies disagreement in the axial
inventory distribution while retaining disagreement in total amount. Spatial
$E_1$ quantifies disagreement in the two-dimensional inventory distribution at
the common-bin scale.

For the two-scale streamwise profiles, we additionally report the
independently normalized shape discrepancy,
\[
    L_{1,\mathrm{shape}} =
    100\sum_b\left|
    \frac{M_b^{\mathrm{DBS}}}{\sum_b M_b^{\mathrm{DBS}}}
    -
    \frac{M_b^{\mathrm{ref}}}{\sum_b M_b^{\mathrm{ref}}}
    \right|,
\]
where $M_b$ is the inventory in a common streamwise bin. This measure
compares the axial distribution after removing differences in total
inventory; the signed inventory differences are reported separately.

\subsection{1D Retardation Benchmark}\label{sec:1D}

The 1D linear-retardation benchmark tests the surface-chemistry coupling on a
uniform domain where the retardation factor has an exact analytical form.
With uniform mobile-water fraction $\theta$ and surface-area density
$a_{\mathrm{surf}}$, the retardation coefficient is
$R = 1 + a_{\mathrm{surf}} K_s / \theta$, and the retarded concentration front
satisfies the advection--diffusion equation with effective velocity
$U_D/(\theta R)$ and effective diffusivity $D/R$. This configuration isolates
the batch calibration of $K_s$, the cell-based surface-capacity term, and the
advective--diffusive transport step. It compares a surface-enabled transport
case, a matched aqueous-only case ($a_{\mathrm{surf}}=0$), and the dilute batch
pair used to estimate $K_s$ by a secant between two equilibrium states.

The retained batch pair gives $K_s=4.253\times10^{-6}$~m and $R=1.851$, so the
surface-enabled front travels at $1/R\approx54\%$ of the aqueous-only front
velocity. The transport mesh contains $80\times1\times1$ cells and uses a
species diffusion coefficient of $10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$. Spatial
profiles at $t=10$~s are compared with the classical semi-infinite
Ogata--Banks solution~\citep{ogata1961solution}. Outlet breakthrough is
compared with a high-resolution finite-domain reference evaluated with the
same fixed-concentration inlet and zero-gradient outlet as the OpenFOAM case.

The mesh- and timestep-refinement study in Online Resource~1 shows
approximately first-order convergence. Relative to the finest combined result,
the reported discretisation gives profile differences of $E_1=3.01\%$ and
$1.05\%$ for the surface-enabled and aqueous-only cases, respectively. These
differences quantify the combined spatial, temporal, and sequential-coupling
errors without conflating them with an unmatched outlet condition.

Figure~\ref{fig:1DRetardation} shows the spatial profiles at $t=10$~s, and
Figure~\ref{fig:1DBreakthrough} shows outlet breakthrough to $t=80$~s. The
surface-enabled front travels at $1/R$ of the aqueous-only velocity, giving
advective breakthrough times of 37.0 and 20.0~s, respectively, consistent with
the calibrated retardation factor. The finite-domain reference captures the
earlier outlet rise caused by the zero-gradient boundary. The semi-infinite
Ogata--Banks curves are also shown: their difference from the finite-domain
reference isolates the effect of the outlet boundary condition.

\subsection{Closed-System Calibration and Batch Balance}

The closed-batch pair serves two purposes. Its two equilibrium states provide
the secant coefficient $K_s=\Delta\Gamma/\Delta C$ used throughout the 1D
benchmark, and each state tests whether one OpenFOAM--PhreeqcRM chemistry update
preserves Ca while repartitioning it between the aqueous and cell-based surface
stores. With no flow, diffusion, or boundary flux, conservation requires
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M} = \theta C + a_{\mathrm{surf}}\Gamma = \mathrm{const},
    \label{eq:closedBalance}
\end{equation}
where $C$ is the aqueous concentration and $\Gamma$ is the adsorbed amount per
reactive area.
Let $C_0$ denote the aqueous concentration at the start of the chemistry update
and $\Gamma_0$ the corresponding initial adsorbed amount per reactive area. In a
consistent SI basis, $C$ is in mol\,m$^{-3}$, $\Gamma$ in mol\,m$^{-2}$, and
$a_{\mathrm{surf}}\Gamma$ in mol\,m$^{-3}$ of bulk volume. OpenFOAM stores the
same fields in kmol\,m$^{-3}$ and kmol\,m$^{-2}$; multiplying both amount fields
by $10^3$ leaves the balance unchanged, and the numerical value of kmol\,m$^{-3}$
is identical to mol\,L$^{-1}$. In the dilute linear limit,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:closedBalance} gives
\begin{equation}
    C_{\mathrm{eq}} =
    \frac{\theta C_0+a_{\mathrm{surf}}\Gamma_0}
         {\theta+a_{\mathrm{surf}}K_s}.
    \label{eq:batchEquil}
\end{equation}

The two calculations use an $80\times1\times1$ mesh, $\theta=0.5$,
$a_{\mathrm{surf}}=10^5\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, and the same Ca$^{2+}$ chemistry on
\texttt{Surf\_a} as the transport benchmark. The scripted initial aqueous
values are $10^{-9}$ and $2\times10^{-9}$~kmol\,m$^{-3}$, numerically equal to
mol\,L$^{-1}$ and equivalent to $10^{-6}$ and $2\times10^{-6}$~mol\,m$^{-3}$
on the SI basis of Eq.~\eqref{eq:closedBalance}. Before the chemistry update,
the surface is initialised from the dilute \texttt{SOLUTION 1} state, giving
$\Gamma_0=1.132\times10^{-19}$~mol\,m$^{-2}$. The retained equilibrium pair
gives $K_s=4.253\times10^{-6}$~m.
Using $\mathcal{M}_0=\theta C_0+a_{\mathrm{surf}}\Gamma_0$, the signed relative
inventory changes are $+8.9\times10^{-10}$ and $-1.1\times10^{-10}$; the maximum
magnitude is below $10^{-9}$. These conservation residuals are calculated from
fields written to 16-digit precision. The test detects errors in the
water-volume, surface-area, amount-unit, and field-mapping conversions across
the OpenFOAM--PhreeqcRM interface, any of which can create or remove field-level
Ca inventory even when PHREEQC executes successfully.

\subsection{Controlled Surface-Area Validation}

The first geometric validation step isolates the construction of
$a_{\mathrm{surf}}$ from transport and chemistry by using analytic curved
interfaces. Two cases are used so that both dimensional branches of
\texttt{smoothSurface} are tested. The 3D branch is evaluated on a voxelised
sphere with reference area $4\pi R^2$; the quasi-2D branch is evaluated on a
circular post with reference area $2\pi R h$, where $h$ is the out-of-plane
thickness. These benchmarks test how closely each image-based surface-area
construction reproduces the intended smooth interface.

The smooth-interface reconstruction recovers a sub-voxel interface before
mapping its area back into the DBS cells. The figure reports four resolutions,
$R/\Delta x = 8$, 16, 32, and 64. For the 3D sphere, using a Gaussian occupancy
width of $\sigma=0.75$ voxels, the \texttt{smoothSurface} errors are
$-2.40\%$, $-0.06\%$, $+0.70\%$, and $+0.87\%$, respectively. The body-fitted
\texttt{snappyHexMesh} comparator remains within approximately $1\%$; its
errors at $R/\Delta x=16$ and 64 are $-0.65\%$ and $-0.50\%$.
For the quasi-2D circular post, using $\sigma=1.5$ voxels, the
\texttt{smoothSurface} contour-length error remains within $0.2\%$ across the
same four resolutions, while the largest \texttt{snappyHexMesh} error is below
$0.1\%$. The circular-cylinder and sphere tests involve different surface
curvatures and body-fitted discretisations, so their \texttt{snappyHexMesh}
errors need not coincide. Neither smooth-interface curve decreases
monotonically to zero.
These controlled tests therefore establish branch- and parameter-specific
bounded error over the plotted range, not convergence or a common accuracy
level for the quasi-2D and 3D constructions.

\subsection{Micromodel Image-Based Surface-Area Comparison}

The image-based test moves from analytic geometry to a reactive micromodel for
which no exact surface-area reference exists. It compares two representations
of the same segmented geometry: a resolved-wall \texttt{snappyHexMesh}
calculation and DBS \texttt{smoothSurface}. The resolved-wall calculation is a
numerical comparator. The comparison tests whether the two surface
representations give similar integrated area and reactive-transport observables
after matching the inlet volumetric flux.

The resolved-wall and DBS results represent the image-derived geometry with
different numerical objects. In the \texttt{snappyHexMesh} case, the geometry
is a body-fitted wall and its face area is mapped to the adjacent chemistry
cells as an explicit surface-area density. In DBS, the same area is obtained
from the image-based smooth-interface reconstruction. Surface complexation in
both cases therefore samples a cell-supported area density together with the
local aqueous concentration. The pressure drops are calibrated separately to
match the inlet volumetric flux. The comparison is made on the integrated
reactive area, its spatial distribution, the aqueous transport profile, and the
resulting sorbed inventory. Because wall faces and Cartesian reconstruction
cells carry area on different supports, a sub-cell displacement can produce
order-unity local relative differences or a zero denominator even when nearby
interfaces and integrated areas agree. Local maps are therefore interpreted as
area redistribution, while totals and common streamwise bins provide the
quantitative comparison.

For the resolved-wall \texttt{snappyHexMesh} reference, the integrated
reactive area is $1.65 \times 10^{-5}$~m$^2$, compared with
$1.67 \times 10^{-5}$~m$^2$ for DBS \texttt{smoothSurface}, an error of
$+1.48\%$. The separately calibrated pressure drops give inlet volumetric
fluxes that agree within $0.004\%$. At $t=200$~s, the dissolved inventory
differs by $-0.07\%$ and the streamwise aqueous-inventory-profile discrepancy is
$E_1=5.11\%$.
The DBS sorbed inventory differs by $+1.68\%$. The integrated area, dissolved
inventory, streamwise profile, and sorbed inventory therefore quantify the
agreement between the two numerical representations; the local area
distributions remain support-dependent.

three-dimensional image crop. A $128^3$-voxel Estaillades subvolume is used,
with the micropore label remapped to solid so that the comparison isolates the
external surface between pore and grain. The two cases use the same

\subsection{Resolved-to-DBS Two-Scale Sorption Benchmark}

The unresolved-matrix component of the DBS model is tested by comparing an
upscaled DBS representation with an aligned, fully resolved micromodel based on
the multiscale images used in the dispersivity study of
\citet{maes2024dispersivity}. The resolved reference uses a high-resolution
crop of the micromodel in which the matrix pore space is explicitly
represented. The DBS calculation uses the corresponding lower-resolution
image of the same physical region, in which the large vugs remain resolved
while the matrix phase is assigned porosity, permeability, effective
dispersion, and intrinsic reactive surface area from the high-resolution
matrix representative elementary volume. The matrix permeability provides
the unresolved flow resistance; the effective permeability of the complete
domain also depends on the geometry and connectivity of the resolved vugs.
For the $6\times6$~mm micromodel examined by
\citet{maes2024dispersivity}, a matrix permeability of
$4.04\times10^{-12}$~$\mathrm{m^2}$ yielded a whole-domain permeability of
$5.10\times10^{-12}$~$\mathrm{m^2}$ in both the fully resolved and DBS
calculations, equal at the reported precision.

Figure~\ref{fig:twoScaleLithiumNestedGeometries} shows the aligned geometry
hierarchy used for this comparison. The upscaled DBS and resolved-reference
transport domains have the same physical size, while the matrix representative
elementary volume provides the unresolved matrix properties used in the DBS
calculation.

The reactive-transport problem is formulated as a critical-mineral recovery
analogue for direct lithium extraction. A Li-bearing Na-K-Mg brine invades an
initially Li-free brine, and Li$^{+}$ competes with the major brine cations for
a generic sorbent site:
\[
    \mathrm{Sor\_aH} + \mathrm{Li}^{+}
        \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Sor\_aLi} + \mathrm{H}^{+}.
\]
Parallel reactions for Na$^{+}$, K$^{+}$, and Mg$^{2+}$ provide competing
surface occupancy. The chemistry is generic, representing a Li-selective
surface-complexation benchmark for engineered sorbent coatings rather than a
specific commercial direct-lithium-extraction material.

The resolved and DBS cases are simulated at matched pore-scale P\'{e}clet number,
\[
    Pe = \frac{U_D L_{\mathrm{pore}}}{D\theta},
\]
where $U_D$ is the Darcy velocity, $L_{\mathrm{pore}}$ is the pore length scale
reported by the flow post-processing, $D$ is the aqueous diffusion coefficient,
and $\theta$ is the porosity. Evaluating $Pe$ using the corresponding
$U_D$, $L_{\mathrm{pore}}$, $D$, and $\theta$ for each case places the
comparison at the same relative strength of advection and molecular diffusion
on the pore length scale. The field comparison shown below evaluates the
spatial Li$^{+}$ aqueous concentration and sorbed $\mathrm{Sor\_aLi}$ amounts
against the fully resolved reference at the same $Pe$ and matched advective
distance $x/L=U_Dt/(\theta L)=0.625$, where $t$ is the elapsed transport time
and $L$ is the common domain length. The quantitative $Pe$ sweep is evaluated
after 1.25 advective transits.

The P\'{e}clet number also controls the accuracy of the two-scale reduction. At
low $Pe$, diffusive exchange can equilibrate the
unresolved matrix over the residence time of the advecting fluid, so the DBS
cell behaves as an effective local reservoir with averaged porosity, diffusion
coefficient, permeability, and intrinsic surface area. At higher $Pe$, solute
can bypass part of the matrix before diffusion has accessed the internal
surface. In that regime, matching the integrated surface area is not sufficient:
the reactive prediction also depends on the time-dependent accessibility of
that area to the aqueous front. The resolved-to-DBS benchmark therefore tests
how well the two-scale reduction preserves surface accessibility as $Pe$
varies.

The choice of surface reconstruction was first tested in two short DBS
calculations at $Pe=1$. Over three matched checkpoints ending at
$0.99564208$~s, the reconstructions differ by at most $0.000647\%$ in
integrated area, $0.000703\%$ in aqueous Li inventory, and $0.2110\%$ in
sorbed Li inventory. The corresponding normalised L1 differences are
$0.00172\%$ and $0.5147\%$. This interval covers only $0.489\%$ of the
planned run, so the comparison establishes sensitivity to the area
reconstruction at early time.

The native-grid fields at $Pe=1$ provide a direct spatial comparison
(Figure~\ref{fig:twoScalePeOneFields}). The DBS solution
reproduces the resolved calculation's advancing aqueous front and the
corresponding sorbed-Li distribution at $x/L=0.625$. The resolved geometry
retains pore-scale concentration variations around individual matrix grains,
whereas the DBS field represents their smooth cell-scale average.

chemistry, the specified intrinsic-area closure reproduces domain-scale uptake
across the tested $Pe$ range.

\subsection{Validation Summary}\label{sec:valSummary}

The validation sequence separates conservative aqueous--surface
state transfer, coupled transport, image-derived surface geometry, and
two-scale DBS sorption upscaling. The closed-batch pair tests the field-level Ca
balance during local equilibration, whereas the transient 1D benchmark tests
the retarded transport response and its mesh and timestep convergence on a
domain with no geometric ambiguity. The controlled sphere and circular-post
benchmarks isolate the geometric accuracy of the interface-area construction
itself. The micromodel solid-grain and 3D
external-surface comparisons transfer that geometric question to reactive image
benchmarks by comparing DBS \texttt{smoothSurface} with resolved-wall
\texttt{snappyHexMesh} references.
The resolved-to-DBS micromodel benchmark tests the two-scale reactive-transport
representation used when part of an image is treated as an unresolved reactive
matrix. Table~\ref{tab:validationSummary} consolidates the quantitative tests;
the geometric validation results are reported in
Figures~\ref{fig:sphereSurfaceValidation}, \ref{fig:hm12SurfaceValidation},
and~\ref{fig:external3DSurfaceValidation}, with global errors in
Tables~\ref{tab:micromodelProfileErrors} and~\ref{tab:external3DValidation};
the two-scale transport results are reported in
Figures~\ref{fig:twoScalePeOneFields} and~\ref{fig:twoScaleErrorVsPe}.
