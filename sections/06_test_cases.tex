\section{Test Cases}

Table~\ref{tab:simParams} lists the full set of simulation parameters for each
test case. Three distinct surface-complexation chemistries are demonstrated,
each chosen to highlight a different aspect of nonlinear multi-species coupling
that cannot be captured by a simple retardation factor, and each motivated by
a distinct class of subsurface application:
(i)~Pb$^{2+}$ sorption on a two-site Dzombak--Morel hydrous ferric oxide (HFO)
coating for the 2D fracture case, representative of heavy-metal retardation in
fractured rock systems and permeable reactive barrier (PRB) remediation;
(ii)~competitive Zn$^{2+}$/Ca$^{2+}$ sorption on a single-site generic surface
for the Kelvin 3D case, representative of trace-metal contamination in
Ca$^{2+}$-bearing groundwater and microporous industrial sorbent media; and
(iii)~Sr$^{2+}$ leachate invading Ca$^{2+}$-bearing limestone formation water,
representing a radwaste deep geological repository (DGR) scenario,
for the Estaillades 3D case.
In all cases the mobile-water threshold is $\theta_{\mathrm{mask}} \le 10^{-3}$
and PHREEQC thermodynamic data are drawn from the GeoChem.dat database
distributed with GeoChemFoam.
The 1D case uses a prescribed uniform inlet velocity $U = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$;
the 2D and 3D image-based cases use a velocity field obtained from a
prior \texttt{simpleDBSFoam} flow calculation with pressure drop
$\Delta P = 5\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{Pa}$ and kinematic viscosity
$\nu = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-1}}$.

\subsection{Simple 2D Image-Based Case: Pb$^{2+}$ on Dzombak--Morel HFO}

The 2D demonstration uses the segmented fracture-network image
\texttt{4StraightFractures} with dimensions
$500 \times 500 \times 1$ and two labels corresponding to micropore ($1$) and
pore ($2$). The computational grid is $250 \times 250 \times 1$.
This case provides a geometrically transparent setting to study heavy-metal
retardation by iron-oxide coatings on fracture walls, directly relevant to
Pb$^{2+}$ and other heavy-metal contaminants migrating through iron-rich
fractured aquifers, acid mine drainage pathways, and engineered permeable
reactive barriers where HFO is the primary sorbent phase.

The chemistry models Pb$^{2+}$ sorption on hydrous ferric oxide (HFO) using the
Dzombak--Morel two-site model \citep{dzombak1990surface}. Strong sites
(Hfo$_s$) have intrinsic $\log K = +4.65$ (near-irreversible uptake at pH 7);
weak sites (Hfo$_w$) have $\log K = +0.3$ (reversible bulk capacity). Site
densities are $2 \times 10^{-7}$~mol~m$^{-2}$ (Hfo$_s$) and
$6 \times 10^{-6}$~mol~m$^{-2}$ (Hfo$_w$). Inlet Pb$^{2+}$ concentration is
$C_{\mathrm{in}} = 10^{-3}$~mol~m$^{-3}$ at pH~7; the initial domain is
essentially Pb-free.

The high conditional affinity of HFO for Pb$^{2+}$ at pH~7 still produces
substantial retardation at this higher loading, but the stronger injection now
drives an observable dissolved plume across most of the domain over the
20000~s run. The reactive interface is the micropore-pore contact, with an
intrinsic unresolved surface-area density
$a_{\mathrm{surf,intr}} = 10^{5}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ assigned to the
$\epsilon = 0.4$ micropore phase.

Figure~\ref{fig:2DFractures} reports the segmented geometry alongside the Pb$^{2+}$/C$_{\mathrm{in}}$ and strong-site occupancy
Hfo$_s$OPb$^+$/Hfo$_{s,\mathrm{tot}}$ at $t = 20000$~s and reveals that Pb$^{2+}$ advances preferentially
along fracture channels (faster transport path) relative to the micropore phase, while
the strongest Pb uptake remains concentrated in reactive micropore cells near the
inlet and around downstream fracture intersections.

\subsection{Simple 3D Image-Based Case: Competitive Zn$^{2+}$/Ca$^{2+}$ Sorption}

The simple 3D case uses a Kelvin-cell image dataset with dimensions
$200 \times 200 \times 200$ and voxel size $10~\mu\mathrm{m}$, where
label $1$ denotes pore and label $2$ denotes micropore.
This case demonstrates competitive multi-species sorption in a fully
three-dimensional unresolved surface network: two dissolved species compete for
the same surface sites, producing coupled nonlinear isotherms that cannot be
described by independent retardation factors.
The scenario is representative of trace-metal transport (e.g.\ Zn$^{2+}$
from industrial or mining sources) in Ca$^{2+}$-bearing groundwater, where
the background divalent cation competes for the same surface sites as the
contaminant and suppresses effective retardation relative to single-species
estimates.

The chemistry uses a single-site \texttt{Surf\_a} model with two competing
reactions:
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{Surf_a H} + \mathrm{Ca^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Surf_a Ca^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -5.85 \\
    \mathrm{Surf_a H} + \mathrm{Zn^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Surf_a Zn^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -4.0
\end{align}
Zn$^{2+}$ binds approximately $70\times$ more strongly than Ca$^{2+}$ per unit
concentration at pH~7. The initial and inlet Ca$^{2+}$ are both
$C_{\mathrm{in}}(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}) = 10^{-4}$~mol~m$^{-3}$; Zn$^{2+}$ enters
only at the inlet at $C_{\mathrm{in}}(\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}) = 10^{-5}$~mol~m$^{-3}$
with zero initial condition. Surface sites are initially empty and load rapidly
with Ca$^{2+}$ on the first PHREEQC step before the Zn$^{2+}$ front arrives.
Site density is $2.4\times10^{-9}$~mol~m$^{-2}$ and
$a_{\mathrm{surf,intr}} = 10^{5}$~m$^{-1}$ in the micropore phase.

Figure~\ref{fig:3DKelvin} shows the geometry and 3D volume renders of both
competing dissolved species and their corresponding surface-loading changes at
$t = 12$~s.
Figure~\ref{fig:KelvinTemporal} shows the temporal evolution of domain-mean
Ca$^{2+}$ and Zn$^{2+}$ (normalised by their respective inlet concentrations)
and the incremental surface loadings \texttt{Surf\_aCa+} and \texttt{Surf\_aZn+}
over the full 120~s simulation. The Zn$^{2+}$ front reaches $C/C_{\mathrm{in}} \approx 1$
within ${\sim}20$~s as the higher-affinity species displaces Ca$^{2+}$ from surface
sites; the Ca$^{2+}$ domain mean remains near unity because of the uniform inlet
background. The surface-loading panel shows the resulting competitive exchange:
Surf\_aZn$^+$ accumulates while Surf\_aCa$^+$ is progressively displaced.

\subsection{Large 3D Porous-Media Case: Sr$^{2+}$ DGR Scenario}

The large 3D porous-medium case uses an Estaillades-style three-phase
segmented image \citep{menke2021upscaling} with dimensions
$500 \times 500 \times 500$ and voxel size $4~\mu\mathrm{m}$. The three phases
correspond to pore ($1$), micropore ($2$), and solid ($3$). The simulation is
run on a $50 \times 50 \times 50$ coarse grid on eight MPI ranks. This case
demonstrates nonlinear chromatographic exchange in a geologically realistic
pore structure, motivated by radionuclide migration in deep geological
repositories (DGR) for nuclear waste.

The chemistry models Sr$^{2+}$ leachate from a waste package invading
Ca$^{2+}$-bearing limestone formation water. Surface sites are pre-equilibrated
with 1~mM Ca$^{2+}$ (via \texttt{surfaceInitialSolutionId~1}) before transport
begins. The two competing surface reactions are:
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{Surf_a H} + \mathrm{Ca^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Surf_a Ca^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -5.85 \\
    \mathrm{Surf_a H} + \mathrm{Sr^{2+}} &\rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Surf_a Sr^{+}} + \mathrm{H^{+}}, \quad \log K = -5.5
\end{align}
Sr$^{2+}$ (site density $2.4\times10^{-9}$~mol~m$^{-2}$) binds slightly more
strongly than Ca$^{2+}$. The initial and outlet-side condition is
Ca$^{2+}$ = $10^{-3}$~mol~m$^{-3}$, Sr$^{2+}$ = 0; the inlet carries
Sr$^{2+}$ = $10^{-4}$~mol~m$^{-3}$, Ca$^{2+}$ = 0 at pH~7.

This produces a classic chromatographic exchange pattern: a Ca$^{2+}$
desorption wave precedes the Sr$^{2+}$ adsorption front, and the effective
retardation of Sr$^{2+}$ is lower than the single-species prediction because
Ca$^{2+}$ still competes for sites ahead of the front. Over the saved output
sequence used here ($t = 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100$~s), the domain-mean
Sr$^{2+}$ rises from approximately $0.13$ to $0.53$ of
$C_{\mathrm{in}}$, while the domain-mean Ca$^{2+}$ falls from approximately
$0.85$ to $0.35$ of $C_{\mathrm{init}}$. Over the same interval,
\mbox{$\Delta$\texttt{Surf\_aSr+}} increases monotonically while
\mbox{$\Delta$\texttt{Surf\_aCa+}} becomes progressively more negative,
quantifying the net replacement of Ca$^{2+}$ by Sr$^{2+}$ on the reactive
surface. Near-solid cells (\mbox{$\theta \le 10^{-3}$}) are masked from the
reactive-surface field.

Figure~\ref{fig:3DEstaillades} shows the geometry and 3D volume renders of the
dissolved and adsorbed fields at $t = 40$~s.
Figure~\ref{fig:EstailladesTemporal} summarises the corresponding domain-mean
exchange history.
